What changed, and why

The earlier draft was organised by application: Copilot in Excel, Copilot in Outlook, Copilot in Word. That is how Microsoft sells it, and it is the wrong way round for your audience. Nobody in finance wakes up wanting to learn Excel Copilot. They wake up with a close to finish and a board pack due Friday.

So the course is now organised by job. The five tasks you named became the five core modules, and the applications appear wherever the job needs them. A single reconciliation lesson moves through Excel, Teams and Outlook because that is how the actual work moves. It makes the course harder to write and considerably more valuable to buy.

Your five red lines

These are your words, unchanged. They are now a data object in the codebase, which means they appear identically everywhere: on the Academy home page, in lesson 1.3 of this course, and in every course we write next. If you ever reword them, they change in one place and update everywhere.

1. Final decisions that require professional judgement, accountability or sign-off, especially where the consequences are material.

2. Making decisions purely based on AI output without checking the underlying data, assumptions, calculations and sources.

3. Handling highly sensitive or confidential financial information in public AI tools without appropriate data controls.

4. Situations where there is no clear audit trail or where you cannot explain how the conclusion was reached.

5. Fully automating high-risk decisions involving compliance, tax, audit, credit, investment or regulatory obligations without appropriate human oversight.

Each module below ends with the specific red line that bites hardest for that task. That is deliberate. A rule stated once in an introduction is forgotten; the same rule met five times, each time attached to a job the learner just did, is retained.

Module 1 · Getting started with Copilot in a finance team

3 lessons · roughly 38 minutes. Sets the boundary before anyone touches client data.

Before you start: which Copilot do you actually have?

This course is written for people whose firm runs Microsoft 365, which is nearly everyone in Australian finance. It is not written on the assumption that you hold a paid Copilot seat, because plenty of firms do not, and the feature level varies between those that do. Take two minutes and find out which of these you are, because it changes how you do the exercises, not whether you can.

Copilot only as a separate chat window or web pageThe chat that comes with most business and enterprise subscriptions, at no extra cost, with commercial data protectionDo every exercise by opening the file and supplying the content yourself. Each lesson tells you how

Microsoft renames these tiers regularly, so if you ask your IT or subscription manager, ask by capability rather than by product name: can I use Copilot inside Word and Excel on our own documents, or only in a chat window? That one question tells you which row you are in.

1.1 What Copilot is, and where it lives in Microsoft 365

What you will be able to do

Find Copilot in each app your team uses, and explain in one sentence what it is doing when you ask it something.

The idea

Copilot is not a separate product you visit. It sits inside Excel, Outlook, Word, Teams and PowerPoint, and it reads what you already have access to in Microsoft 365. That last part is the whole story: Copilot's answers are only as good as the file, the thread or the meeting it is looking at, and it can only look at things you are already permitted to see.

Do this

Open a workbook you know well. Click the Copilot button in the Home ribbon. Ask it something you already know the answer to:

Summarise what this worksheet contains, in five bullet points, and tell me which columns look like calculated fields.

Starting with a question you can already answer is the fastest way to calibrate how much to trust it.

No Copilot button? You are in the second row of the panel above. Open the Copilot chat instead, attach or paste the same worksheet, and ask the same question. The lesson works identically. The only thing you are missing is not having to hand it the file.

Watch out

Copilot answers confidently whether or not it has understood the file. Confidence is not accuracy, and this is the single most expensive habit to unlearn.

Takeaway

Copilot reads your work. It does not know your business.

1.2 The data boundary: what Copilot can and cannot see

What you will be able to do

State exactly which data your Copilot licence can reach, and decide confidently whether a given client file may be put in front of it.

The idea

There is a meaningful difference between Microsoft 365 Copilot working inside your tenant on files you already have access to, and a free consumer chatbot you paste text into. In the first, the material stays inside your organisation's boundary and inherits your existing permissions. In the second, you have moved client data outside the firm. Most people cannot articulate this difference, which is why they either use AI recklessly or refuse to use it at all.

Do this

Take the three files you touched most this week. For each, write one line: where it lives, who else can see it, and whether it contains anything you would not email to an external party. Keep the list. Module 7 turns it into a rule for your team.

Your input: which Copilot licence tier your target firms realistically have. The screenshots and the "can it see this" answer differ between Microsoft 365 Copilot and the free Copilot chat, and I want the examples to match what your buyers can actually click. If you are unsure, I will write it for Microsoft 365 Copilot and add a one-page note for firms on the free tier.
Watch out

A file being inside your tenant does not make it appropriate to process. Permission and appropriateness are two different tests, and only one of them is technical.

Red line 3. Handling highly sensitive or confidential financial information in public AI tools without appropriate data controls. If you cannot say where the data goes, do not put it there.

1.3 The five red lines: where AI does not belong in finance

What you will be able to do

Recognise, before you start a task, whether AI belongs in it at all.

The idea

Every other AI course tells you what the tool can do. This lesson tells you where to stop, and it comes third rather than last on purpose. A professional who knows the limits uses the tool more, not less, because they are no longer nervous about the parts that matter.

The five limits are taught as a decision you make at the start of a task, not a review you do at the end. By the time you are checking, you have already committed.

Do this

Take the five red lines and apply them to your own week. Write down one task where AI clearly belongs, one where it clearly does not, and one you are genuinely unsure about. The uncertain one is the interesting one, and we return to it in Module 7.

Watch out

The most common failure is not using AI where it was forbidden. It is using it where it was fine, and then not checking, because the output looked finished.

Takeaway

Decide whether AI belongs before you start, not after you have an answer you like.

Knowledge check, end of Module 1. A client sends a spreadsheet of transactions and asks whether a deduction is allowable. Which part is safe to give Copilot?

A. The whole question, then send the answer
B. Summarising and categorising the transactions, while the deductibility conclusion stays with you
C. Neither, AI cannot help here

Answer: B. Copilot does the volume work. The judgement, and the accountability for it, does not move.

Module 2 · Bank reconciliation

3 lessons · roughly 42 minutes. The first real job, chosen because it is high volume, low judgement, and everybody has one.

2.1 Preparing a bank statement and ledger Copilot can read

What you will be able to do

Get two messy exports into a shape Copilot can actually work with, in under five minutes.

The idea

Most reconciliation frustration with AI is not an AI problem. It is a data shape problem. Copilot works well on a clean table with headers in row one, one row per transaction, real dates and real numbers. It works badly on a bank export with three header rows, merged cells, and amounts stored as text with a currency symbol. Ten minutes of shaping saves an hour of arguing.

Do this

Put the bank export on one sheet and the ledger on another, then ask:

This sheet is a bank export. Tell me which row the real headers start on, which columns are dates, which are amounts, and flag any column where the values are stored as text rather than as numbers or dates. Do not change anything yet.

Note the "do not change anything yet". Ask what is wrong before you ask for a fix, so you stay in control of the edit.

Check this

Sum the amount column yourself and compare it to the statement's closing figure. If those two do not agree, nothing downstream can be trusted, and you have found it in ten seconds rather than at the end.

Watch out

Dates are where Australian teams get burned. A file exported with United States conventions turns the 3rd of April into the 4th of March silently, and the reconciliation will still balance. Always spot check one date you recognise.

Takeaway

Shape the data first. Everything after that gets easier.

2.2 Matching transactions and surfacing the exceptions

What you will be able to do

Have Copilot do the matching pass, and get a clean list of exceptions instead of a wall of ticks.

The idea

The valuable output of a reconciliation is not the matched items. It is the unmatched ones. So do not ask Copilot to reconcile. Ask it to isolate what does not agree, and to tell you why it thinks so.

Do this
Compare the transactions in the Bank sheet with those in the Ledger sheet. Match on amount and date within three days. Return three tables: matched, in the bank but not the ledger, and in the ledger but not the bank. For each unmatched item include the date, amount, description, and your best guess at the reason. Do not delete or alter any source rows.

Three tables, not one verdict. The shape of the request determines whether you get something reviewable.

Check this

Two checks, both fast. First, matched plus unmatched must equal the total row count on each side; if rows have gone missing, the answer is wrong regardless of how tidy it looks. Second, pick the largest matched item and verify it by eye.

Watch out

A three-day tolerance will happily match two genuinely different payments of the same amount, which is common with regular supplier payments. Any match you cannot explain is an exception, not a match.

Red line 2. Making decisions purely based on AI output without checking the underlying data, assumptions, calculations and sources. A reconciliation that balances because the tool was allowed to drop rows is worse than one that does not balance, because nobody goes looking.

2.3 Explaining and clearing the unmatched items

What you will be able to do

Turn a list of exceptions into a short written explanation your manager or client can act on.

The idea

The last mile of a reconciliation is writing up what is outstanding and why. It is the part that gets rushed at 6pm, and the part a reviewer actually reads. Copilot is genuinely good here, because you are asking it to describe data you have already verified rather than to reach a conclusion.

Do this
Using only the unmatched table above, write a short note for the file. Group the items into: timing differences, likely missing entries, and items needing investigation. For each group give the count, the total value, and one sentence on what needs to happen next. Plain professional English, no bullet point jargon.
Check this

Read the note against the exception table line by line. Copilot will occasionally state a total that does not match its own table, and a reviewer will spot it before you do.

Your input: one real, de-identified reconciliation with a handful of genuine exceptions in it, ideally including one that turned out to be an error rather than timing. A worked example built on a real mess is worth more than any explanation, and it is the single asset that would most improve this module.
Takeaway

Ask AI to describe what you verified. Do not ask it to conclude what you have not.

Module 3 · Month-end close

3 lessons · roughly 44 minutes. From a checklist nobody maintains to a close that explains itself.

3.1 Building a close checklist Copilot can drive

What you will be able to do

Turn the close that currently lives in one person's head into a written checklist, and use Copilot to keep it moving.

The idea

Most closes run on institutional memory. That is a risk in itself, and it also means AI cannot help, because there is nothing written down for it to work from. Writing the checklist is therefore the first real AI task, and Copilot is good at drafting it from what already exists.

Do this

Point Copilot at last month's close folder and the Teams channel where the chasing happened:

From these files and this channel, reconstruct the steps we actually performed in last month's close, in order. For each step list who did it, what it depended on, and where it got stuck. Mark any step that appears to have been done twice or skipped.

Reconstructing the real process is far more useful than drafting an ideal one, because the real one is what your team will recognise.

Check this

Show the reconstructed list to whoever runs the close. Their corrections are the most valuable part of the exercise, and they take five minutes.

Takeaway

You cannot automate a process you have never written down.

3.2 Accruals, prepayments and the supporting workings

What you will be able to do

Draft recurring journals and their supporting workings faster, with the calculation visible rather than hidden.

The idea

An accrual is a small calculation plus a paper trail. The calculation is easy; the trail is what takes the time and what an auditor asks for. Copilot can produce both, provided you insist on seeing the working rather than the answer.

Do this
From the attached invoice schedule, calculate the accrual required at 31 August for services received but not yet invoiced. Show the calculation for each supplier as a separate row: supplier, period covered, daily rate, days accrued, amount. Then give the total. Do not round until the total.
Check this

Recalculate two rows by hand, one large and one small. The small one matters: rounding and part-period errors hide in immaterial lines and then repeat every month.

Watch out

Copilot will fill a gap with a plausible assumption rather than telling you the data was missing. If a supplier's period is unclear, it may simply assume a full month. Ask it explicitly to list any assumption it made, then check that list.

List every assumption you made and every field you inferred rather than read directly from the data.
Takeaway

Always ask what it assumed. The answer is rarely nothing.

3.3 Flux analysis: explaining every movement worth explaining

What you will be able to do

Produce a month-on-month movement analysis with draft explanations, ready for you to confirm or correct.

The idea

Flux analysis is repetitive, and it is where a close either becomes insightful or becomes a formality. Copilot can identify every movement above a threshold and propose a reason from the underlying detail. You confirm the reasons, which is the part that requires knowing the business.

Do this
Compare this month's trial balance with last month's. List every account where the movement is greater than 10 percent or 5,000 dollars, whichever is smaller. For each, give the movement in dollars and percent, and propose the most likely explanation based on the transactions in that account. Mark each explanation as confirmed or unconfirmed. Everything you propose is unconfirmed.
Check this

Work down the unconfirmed column and convert each one to confirmed, corrected or unknown. The unknowns are your real work, and there are usually fewer than you expect.

Red line 4. Situations where there is no clear audit trail or where you cannot explain how the conclusion was reached. If an explanation reaches the board and nobody can say where it came from, it should not have been in the pack.

Knowledge check, end of Module 3. Copilot proposes that a 40,000 dollar rise in professional fees was "due to increased advisory activity". What do you do?

A. Include it, it is plausible
B. Delete it, AI cannot explain movements
C. Open the account, find the actual invoices, and either confirm it or write the real reason

Answer: C. It is a starting hypothesis, not a finding. Plausible and true are different things, and the board cannot tell them apart.

Module 4 · FP&A

3 lessons · roughly 45 minutes. Where the work stops being mechanical and judgement starts to matter.

4.1 Turning last year's actuals into a first-cut budget

What you will be able to do

Produce a defensible first-cut budget in an afternoon instead of a fortnight, with every driver stated.

The idea

A first-cut budget is mostly arithmetic applied to assumptions. The arithmetic is Copilot's job. The assumptions are yours, and the discipline is refusing to let them be invented. So you supply the drivers explicitly rather than asking for a budget.

Do this
Build a 12 month budget from the actuals in this sheet, using exactly these drivers and no others: - revenue grows 6 percent on prior year, spread on the same seasonal pattern as last year - salaries rise 3.5 percent from 1 July - all other costs rise with revenue except rent and insurance, which are fixed Show each line as prior year, driver applied, budget. If any line does not fit these rules, list it separately rather than guessing.

"List it separately rather than guessing" is the most useful sentence in this course. It converts silent invention into a visible question.

Check this

Check the total, then check the seasonality. A model that hits the right annual number with the wrong shape will pass a glance and fail the first cash-flow conversation.

Your input: the drivers a typical client of yours actually uses. I have written generic ones. Real drivers, even from one anonymised client, would make this lesson unmistakably expert rather than merely competent.
Takeaway

Give it your assumptions. Never let it choose them.

4.2 Scenarios and sensitivities without breaking the model

What you will be able to do

Build best, base and downside cases from one model, and explain what actually drives the difference.

The idea

Boards do not want a number, they want a range and the reason for it. Copilot is quick at generating variants, and the risk is that you end up with three spreadsheets nobody can reconcile. The rule is one model, one set of switchable assumptions, three outputs.

Do this
Using the budget on this sheet, build three scenarios that change only the assumptions block, never the formulas: - base: as built - downside: revenue growth 0 percent, debtor days up by 15 - upside: revenue growth 12 percent, headcount up by two from January For each, show revenue, EBITDA and closing cash. Then tell me which single assumption moves closing cash the most.
Check this

Confirm the base scenario still reproduces your original budget exactly. If it does not, a formula was changed somewhere and every scenario is now unreliable.

Watch out

Copilot may hard-code a value where a formula belonged. The model keeps working and quietly stops responding to the driver. Spot-check that changing one assumption actually moves the outputs.

Takeaway

Change assumptions, never formulas. Then prove the base case still ties.

4.3 Variance commentary in your CFO's voice

What you will be able to do

Draft variance commentary that sounds like your organisation and survives review.

The idea

Commentary is where AI writing is most obvious and most irritating, because the default output is padded and hedged. The fix is to give it a real sample of your own previous commentary and instruct it to match, then to be specific about what a good explanation contains.

Do this
Here is our commentary from the last two months. Match its length, tone and level of detail exactly. Using the variance table below, draft this month's commentary. Rules: name the driver, quantify it, say whether it is timing or permanent, and state what we are doing about it. No adjectives that do not carry information. Anything you cannot support from the table, leave as [TO CONFIRM].
Check this

Search the draft for [TO CONFIRM] and resolve every one. If there are none at all, be suspicious rather than pleased.

Red line 1. Final decisions that require professional judgement, accountability or sign-off. Commentary is not neutral description. It tells the board where to look, and that framing is your professional judgement, not the tool's.

Module 5 · The board pack and management pack

3 lessons · roughly 41 minutes. The most visible thing finance produces, and the most time-consuming.

5.1 From numbers to a narrative the board will read

What you will be able to do

Decide what belongs in the pack before you build any of it.

The idea

Most board packs are long because nobody decided what to leave out. The discipline is to write the three things the board must take away first, then build only what supports them. Copilot helps by drafting candidate messages from the numbers, which you then cut.

Do this
From the management accounts and the variance commentary attached, propose the five most decision-relevant messages for a board, ranked. For each: the message in one sentence, the evidence behind it, and the decision or action it should prompt. Exclude anything that is interesting but does not require a board-level decision.
Check this

Ask whether anything in the top five would surprise the chair. If nothing would, the pack is describing rather than informing, and it is worth another pass.

Takeaway

Decide the message first. The pack is evidence for it, not a substitute.

5.2 Building the pack in Word and PowerPoint

What you will be able to do

Turn agreed messages plus supporting schedules into a formatted pack, without retyping anything.

The idea

This is the one part of the job where Copilot's convenience features genuinely shine: converting a document into a deck, carrying a house template through, and rewriting the same content for two audiences. It is also where errors are introduced silently, because numbers get retyped into slides.

Do this
Turn this board paper into a 10 slide deck using our template. One message per slide as the headline. Put supporting numbers in the slide body, and move all detailed schedules to an appendix. Do not re-type any figure: reference the source table so I can check each one.
Check this

Take the six most important figures on the slides and tie each back to the source schedule. Six is usually enough to establish whether the conversion was faithful.

Watch out

A number transcribed into a slide has left its audit trail behind. Wherever the tool allows a live link to the source, use it.

Your input: a board or management pack structure you would stand behind, even just the contents page. Every firm has a house style and yours will carry authority. I will build the walkthrough around your structure rather than a generic one.

5.3 Anticipating the questions the board will ask

What you will be able to do

Walk into the meeting having already prepared for the three hardest questions.

The idea

This is the highest-value ten minutes in the whole course, and almost nobody does it. Ask Copilot to read your own pack adversarially, as a sceptical director would. It is good at this precisely because it has no loyalty to the work.

Do this
You are a non-executive director with a finance background, reading this pack for the first time. List the ten questions you would ask, hardest first. For each, note whether the pack already answers it, answers it partially, or does not answer it at all.
Check this

Anything marked "does not answer" is either a gap in the pack or a question you need a response ready for. Both are useful; neither is comfortable.

Takeaway

Let AI attack your work before the board does.

Knowledge check, end of Module 5. Copilot builds your deck and one slide shows EBITDA 40,000 dollars higher than the management accounts. Most likely cause?

A. The board pack is correct and the accounts are wrong
B. A figure was transcribed or a subtotal was recalculated during conversion
C. Rounding

Answer: B. Conversion between formats is where numbers drift. This is why you tie the key figures back to source every time.

Module 6 · The real-time dashboard

2 lessons · roughly 29 minutes. The one that changes how the rest of the business sees finance.

6.1 Designing a dashboard around the decisions it serves

What you will be able to do

Choose the six or seven measures that belong on a dashboard, and defend leaving the rest off.

The idea

Dashboards fail for one reason: they are built from what is easy to measure rather than from what someone will decide. Start with the decision, then the measure, then the data. Copilot is a good sounding board for this because it will happily challenge a metric that serves no decision.

Do this
Our operations manager needs to decide weekly on stock purchasing and casual rostering. Propose the smallest set of measures that supports exactly those two decisions. For each, say what action a change in it should trigger. Then list the measures we currently report that do not support either decision.
Check this

For each proposed measure, name the person who will act on it. Any measure without a name attached comes off the dashboard.

Takeaway

A measure nobody acts on is decoration.

6.2 Building it, and keeping it refreshing on its own

What you will be able to do

Build a working dashboard that updates without anyone rebuilding it each month.

The idea

The difference between a dashboard and a report is that nobody touches a dashboard between refreshes. That means the data has to arrive in a consistent shape every period, which is a discipline question more than a technical one.

Do this
Build a summary sheet from the transactions table with these measures: [your list from 6.1]. Use formulas that reference the whole table so new rows are picked up automatically. Do not paste any values. Then tell me exactly what would break if next month's export has an extra column or renamed headers.

That last question is the important one. Knowing the failure mode in advance is what stops a dashboard quietly going stale.

Check this

Add three fake rows to the source table and confirm every measure moves. Then delete them.

Red line 5. Fully automating high-risk decisions without appropriate human oversight. A dashboard that refreshes itself is fine. A dashboard that triggers a payment, a credit decision or a lodgement without a person in the loop is not.

Module 7 · Getting it right every time

1 lesson · roughly 15 minutes. Pulls the whole course into one page the learner keeps.

7.1 Verification, source trails and the pre-send checklist

What you will be able to do

Apply one checklist to any AI-assisted finance output before it leaves your desk, and know what to keep on file.

The idea

Across five very different jobs, the same four habits kept appearing: shape the data first, state the assumptions explicitly, verify a sample by hand, and record what the tool did. That is the whole course in one line, and this lesson turns it into a checklist the learner downloads.

The pre-send checklist
  1. Can I explain how this number was produced, without opening the tool?
  2. Have I verified at least one item by hand, including one small one?
  3. Did I ask what it assumed, and did I check that list?
  4. Does anything here require professional judgement or sign-off that I have not personally given?
  5. If someone asks in six months where this came from, is the trail on the file?
The Australian frame. The National AI Plan's direction is adoption with appropriate safeguards rather than avoidance. That is precisely what this checklist is: it lets a finance team use these tools on real client work while keeping the explainability and human accountability their professional obligations already demand. The plan describes the destination; the checklist is how a finance team gets there on a Tuesday.
Your input: the checklist above is mine. Please edit it in your own words, and add anything you would refuse to sign off without. Your version becomes the downloadable asset carrying your name, so it should sound like you rather than like me.
Takeaway

Copilot drafts. You verify. The file shows who decided.

Final quiz, sample questions

Ten questions, 80 percent to pass, unlimited attempts, and passing unlocks the certificate in this subject. Three samples so you can judge the level.

Q1. You ask Copilot to accrue for services received but not invoiced. It returns a total with no working. What is the correct next step?

A. Post it, the total is what matters
B. Ask it to show the calculation per supplier, then recalculate two rows yourself
C. Post it and note in the file that Copilot prepared it

Answer: B. C is worse than A, because it records reliance without establishing it was reasonable.

Q2. A client emails a spreadsheet containing individuals' salary details and asks for a summary. Your firm has Microsoft 365 Copilot. What is defensible?

A. Paste it into a free public chatbot, it is faster
B. Work on it in the firm's own Microsoft 365 environment, where the file already sits under existing permissions
C. Refuse, salary data can never be processed with AI

Answer: B. C sounds cautious but is wrong, and a rule that strict just drives people to option A quietly.

Q3. Your dashboard refreshes automatically. Which of these should NOT be automated end to end?

A. Recalculating debtor days each morning
B. Releasing supplier payments when the cash measure passes a threshold
C. Emailing the operations manager when stock cover falls below two weeks

Answer: B. A and C inform a person. B replaces one.
